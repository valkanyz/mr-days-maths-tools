Sample record

A field study in three steps

  1. Build. Choose a habitat, dimensions, richness and density. Generate a map. Reusing the same seed and settings recreates the same ecosystem.
  2. Sample. Use random quadrats for population estimates, or choose locations to investigate selection bias. Draw transects across a habitat gradient.
  3. Compare. Reveal the true population, collect more samples and watch the estimates change. Export observations for class analysis.

What counts?

Quadrats and belt transects count organisms whose centres lie inside. Quadrats include top/left edges and exclude bottom/right edges. Line transects record any organism whose circular footprint touches the line, so large organisms are more likely to be recorded.

Random quadrats are selected without replacement from a grid that covers the whole map. Each square has an equal chance of selection. Belt and manual samples may be biased. Overlapping transects can count an organism more than once; sampled area is total effort, not unique coverage.

These are simplified, stationary ecosystems. Colours, shapes, sizes and distribution habits represent fictional species, not a predictive ecological model.
